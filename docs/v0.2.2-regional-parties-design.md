# Proposal: Campaign Influence, Event Weights, and News Balance

**Audience:** Design agent preparing a future Battle for America update.  
**Status:** Owner-requested goals with proposed design and calibration rules. This is a proposal, not an implementation handoff or evidence that the targets have been achieved.  
**Reference inspected:** `build-0.2.0-integration-worktree/build35`, the 0.2.0 candidate identified as unreleased in the 0.2.1 design. Confirm the update's actual base and reconcile later work before incorporating this proposal. Do not assume this snapshot is the final 0.2.1 build.

## Owner's requested outcomes

1. A good player can move the national environment about **five percentage points toward their party** through campaign play, independently of events. The impact should remain modest.
2. Events with at least one **strictly good option** should appear about **50% more often**. Increase their weights slowly and ensure no individual event more than doubles in frequency.
3. News stories should slightly favor the player, at **60/40**. Test the news change in two-party mode only.

The numbers and rules below are initial design choices for the design agent to refine and calibrate; they are not claims about observed results. Keep these three requests as separate entries in the update ledger and do not add unrelated historical audit findings to scope.

## Relevant source observations

- `src/lib/environment.js` stores the generic ballot by state, using Democratic-minus-Republican signs. `genericAverage` aggregates state values using modeled ballots; the displayed `natGeneric` is derived from those values. `applyNationalGenericSwing` redistributes a national change by partisan lean and issue agreement, while respecting state bounds.
- Rally effects already reach the state generic ballot. Weekly `decayGeneric` multiplies state environments by 0.94. Campaign influence must therefore be measured as an incremental change, with existing rally effects and decay accounted for.
- `src/lib/gameEngine.js` builds a legal, deduplicated event pool in `nextEventFor` and draws uniformly. It filters used and unavailable events and supports both player and AI actors. Some event paths are scheduled or otherwise outside this draw.
- `src/lib/nationalNews.js` chooses weighted national cards after filtering recent stories and arc eligibility. Follow-ups and pending rival-incident stories take precedence. `src/lib/worldNews.js` deals local issue stories for individual states and calculates their effects from issue fit. News also has campaign and opponent story paths. Determine the complete player-visible two-party news inventory on the chosen base before changing weights.
- The reference snapshot's normal default is 14 weeks, but campaign length can vary. Existing values and active changes may differ in the selected update base.

## 1. Player influence on the national environment

### Proposed mechanic

Add a **campaign persuasion contribution** to the existing state generic-ballot model. It is earned by the player's voluntary campaign actions and applied through the established national swing path, so the display, state environments, downballot systems, issue alignment, and partisan lean continue to agree. It is a change in the generic-ballot **margin**, not a five-point increase to each party's vote share.

Initial calibration: target roughly **+5.0 player-relative generic-ballot margin points at the end of a standard 14-week campaign** for a strong, varied strategy, relative to the same strategy with the new contribution disabled. Accept a mean incremental result of +4.5 to +5.5 across the calibration matrix. This is a target, not a promise that a player can force the displayed value to rise five points: news, the opponent, decay, and existing mechanics may move it in either direction.

Use successful, player-initiated activities that plausibly reach beyond one state: campaign stops, town halls, nationally distributed advertising, and policy or message actions if they exist and have a resolved outcome. Do not grant credit for simply spending money, fundraising, volunteer drives, selecting a staff recommendation, an event choice, a news effect, a free event-granted action, or an action that fails or is canceled. A debate itself should not add this credit: it can be event-driven. Confirm the exact eligible action IDs and resolution receipts on the chosen base before specifying the implementation.

Start with the following **calibration parameters**, and tune them based on simulation rather than promising these values will already yield five points:

- For each eligible completed action, award 0.12 influence points for a normal success, 0.18 for a strong success, and 0 for failure, a canceled action, or a negative outcome. Use the actual action outcome, not an extra random roll.
- Cap newly earned influence at 0.45 points per week and a campaign lifetime total of 7.0 points. This is a cap on the **new mechanic's earned credit**, not on the national environment or its other sources.
- After the first credited use of the same action family in a week, give later uses in that family half credit. This makes varied strategy more useful without prohibiting repeated actions.
- Apply each credit once at the same completed-action transaction as its existing effects. Use Democratic-positive and Republican-negative signs; pass the relevant issue to the established distribution helper where available. Keep the existing state-level action effects; do not apply the same new credit twice.
- Let the contribution experience the existing weekly state-environment decay. Maintain a separate attribution ledger for new credit, including applied amounts, decay, and state-bound clipping, so the five-point target is evaluated on realized incremental impact rather than lifetime points earned. Save the ledger, weekly totals, and action receipts; reset the weekly count once per week. Old saves start with zero new contribution and do not retroactively receive credit.

Only a completed **player** action earns this new credit. Existing AI action effects remain in place. The target is incremental player agency; track the opponent's existing effects separately rather than crediting them to the player. Preserve scheduled event effects, existing action outcomes, funds, fatigue, and electoral rules. Do not add a second hidden vote-share bonus.

The generic environment is a Democratic-versus-Republican axis. For Green, Libertarian, independent, or regional-party players, do not claim that moving that axis toward either major party favors the player. Return a player-relative third-party model to design before extending this mechanic to those players. Preserve modes that intentionally ignore the national environment.

### Calibration and acceptance

Use paired campaigns or deterministic replays with identical starting states, strategy, and separate random streams: one with the new credit enabled and one disabled. Hold events and news fixed or suppress both for the isolated influence measurement. Compare the final ballot-weighted generic environment from the player's perspective. The action history must be the same in both arms. Include both major parties, multiple starting environments and campaign lengths, varied strong strategies, ordinary play, repetitive play, and minimal eligible action play.

The strong-strategy average should reach +4.5 to +5.5 in the 14-week reference campaign; document its spread, not just its mean. Ordinary and repetitive strategies should earn less than a strong varied strategy; unsuccessful or no eligible actions should earn zero from this mechanic. Report state-level clipping and show that the UI aggregate equals the state-weighted value. Check sign symmetry, weekly limits, final-week actions, save/resume, and interaction with existing decay. Test action opportunity costs and combined campaign balance rather than tuning an isolated counter alone. If the selected base cannot distinguish outcome quality reliably, return the affected action classification to design instead of fabricating a success receipt.

## 2. More events with a strictly good option

### Classification

Classify each **currently legal and available player-facing choice** from the player's perspective, after resolving its target, affordability, conditions, and all possible branches. A choice is strictly good when it has at least one positive gameplay effect and **every possible branch has no harmful effect or harmful risk**. A guaranteed benefit paired with an optional or possible scandal is not strictly good. An opponent penalty is positive for the player; a cost in cash, actions, faction support, momentum, or an effect that can benefit the opponent is harmful. A zero-effect branch is neutral, but a choice with only neutral outcomes is not strictly good. Include delayed, static, and conditional effects and classify uncertain or unsupported semantics conservatively as **unclassified**, not positive. Produce a reviewable event-ID and choice-ID classification report with reasons; do not infer polarity from event title or text.

An event belongs to the target group if at least one available choice qualifies. Recompute if availability or acting-party perspective changes. The request concerns the player-facing experience: do not use the player's favorable classification to bias the AI's draws. Preserve explicit triggers, affordability, no-repeat constraints, scheduled interrupt ordering, and the normal weekly event count.

### Weighted draw and calibration

After building the eligible distinct pool, replace the uniform random draw for **unscheduled player event slots** with a weighted draw. Start each baseline weight at 1; multiply target-group event weights by a global factor `m`, starting at 1.00. All other event weights remain 1. Use the existing slot's seeded random source. Scheduled, mandatory, or forced chain events remain in their existing paths and should be reported separately; changing their cadence or chain order needs a separate owner decision.

Increase `m` in small steps of 0.05 up to 2.00. At each step, replay a representative set of legal pools and full campaigns with common seeds, and measure the **actual presentation rate** of target-group events relative to the baseline. Target approximately a **1.50 ratio**, not a 50-percentage-point increase. The event cap applies to actual per-event presentation frequency across the same representative campaign population: no individual ID may exceed 2.00 times its baseline rate. Also record per-pool selection probabilities so a rare event cannot hide an excessive increase behind sampling noise. Preserve eligible pool composition, timing, and legal constraints when comparing; continue a separate full-campaign check because changed event history can change later eligibility.

Feasibility matters. In one fixed pool with baseline target-group fraction `p`, weighting only that group by `m` makes its appearance-rate ratio `m / (1 - p + m*p)`. For example, if `p = 0.50`, even `m = 2` gives only 1.33 times the baseline target-group rate. Do not claim a 50% increase if the data cannot reach it within the limit. Stop at the highest safe tested weight and report the measured shortfall, the limiting condition, and eligible alternatives for owner review. Do not silently alter triggers, create new events, lower other weights beyond the proposed draw rule, or exceed the cap to hit the target.

Use enough paired seeded trials to estimate the group rate with a 95% confidence interval of approximately ±2 percentage points or better; report counts and confidence intervals for individual IDs. ## 3. News: 60/40 player-favorable

### Proposed interpretation and mechanism

Neutral and genuinely mixed stories remain eligible and are reported separately, not counted in either side of the denominator. This is a frequency target, not a promise that 60% of the total magnitude of news effects will favor the player.

Classify relative to the player's actual major party and the resolved story's gameplay effects and target states. Use the existing resolver's direction and relevant issue fit; do not infer effect from headline sentiment or invert a story's content or consequences. Where one story has material effects in both directions and no unambiguous overall direction, label it mixed and exclude it from the directional ratio. Document classification by story ID and context. Do not count salience alone as an inherently favorable effect.

Apply the bias at the ordinary random-draw boundary for each player-visible two-party news pool: retain eligible neutral/mixed story weights and original effects, and rebalance favorable versus unfavorable eligible candidates using their existing relative weights to achieve the desired 60/40 directional ratio where both pools exist. Respect state relevance, recent-memory rules, no-repeat, arc conditions, and existing story effects. If only one directional side exists, draw from the legal pool; never invent or flip a story. Existing scheduled follow-ups and incident-driven stories keep their established precedence and are included in the overall measured presentation results, even though they are not forced into the random-draw ratio. If that makes the overall 60/40 result infeasible, report it and return the tradeoff to design rather than suppressing stories or overriding eligibility.

Audit which national, state/local, and campaign/opponent news paths actually produce player-visible stories on the selected base. Keep each pool's baseline within-side weights and use separate seeded streams for the calibration harness. Existing `Math.random` paths may need injectable RNG for reproducible comparison without changing normal play. Preserve story effects, frequency, arcs, causality, and offline behavior. Four-party news is outside this test and change; do not route its separate logic through the two-party weighting rule. For a third-party player or another ambiguous mode, preserve existing selection until a player-relative polarity model is approved.

**Reference inspected:** `build-0.2.0-integration-worktree/build35`, a 0.2.0 candidate identified as unreleased in the 0.2.1 design. 3. The presidential race should start from the national environment plus the candidates' modifiers.
4. Later in the campaign, the presidential race should exert coattails that pull downballot races toward the leading presidential party.

## What the existing source shows

Week-two changes must be accounted for by the specified mechanics and their normal weekly timing.

## 2. Separate the opening environment from the presidential race

Treat the selected national environment as the opening **Democratic-minus-Republican generic-ballot margin**. Build the state generic values from it and the existing state configuration, centered so the ballot-weighted national aggregate equals the selected value to within 0.01 point. Candidate identity, VP choice, candidate strengths, presidential polling noise, faction effects belonging to the presidential campaign, and the player's choice of party must not alter that starting generic value.

Calculate the opening presidential race from the same environmental foundation plus the candidates' existing national, regional, state, and applicable running-mate modifiers, applied once for both parties. Keep state fundamentals and their existing electorate weights so the national popular-vote result is aggregated from states. Candidate-specific variation belongs in the presidential race; do not write it back into the opening generic ballot. Maintain normalized party shares and distinguish a margin-point change from a vote-share-point change.

For an isolated two-party control with neutralized state deviations, no third-party vote, no other modifiers or noise, and a D+2 generic environment, if the Democratic candidate contributes +3 and the Republican candidate contributes +1 on the same margin scale, the presidential margin is **D+4**: `+2 + 3 - 1`. In a real campaign, regional/state candidate modifiers and state weights create additional variation; the displayed national vote should not be forced to a simple arithmetic answer that disregards the voter model.

Audit existing starting policy, faction, momentum, VP, ticket, and demographic contributions. ## 4. Presidential coattails from week two onward

### Proposed model

Starting in week two, calculate a modest, symmetric **state-level coattail component** from the difference between the presidential race and a counterfactual presidential race driven by the same generic environment and state fundamentals but without ticket-specific or presidential-campaign contributions. This measures the president's extra strength or weakness in each state, rather than counting the national generic ballot a second time. The gap is Democratic-minus-Republican, so a Democratic advantage pulls downballot results Democratic and a Republican advantage pulls them Republican, regardless of which party the player controls.

For each state `s`, propose:

```text
presidentialGap[s] = actualPresidentialMargin[s] - counterfactualMargin[s]
coattailTarget[s]  = clamp(0.20 * presidentialGap[s], -3.0, +3.0)
coattailNext[s]    = coattailPrevious[s] + 0.25 * (coattailTarget[s] - coattailPrevious[s])
```

These coefficients are starting **design estimates**, not calibrated results. Recalculate once per weekly boundary, beginning when week two opens, from a stable snapshot taken before applying the new coattail. Set the initial component to zero. A constant D+10 presidential gap produces a D+2 target and a D+0.5 coattail on its first weekly update; a constant R+10 gap produces the mirror image. The component approaches its target gradually, reverses as the race changes, and does not grow without bound. Store the full component, and apply only its difference from the previous value. No action, screen render, forecast simulation, or save/load may re-award the same week.

The counterfactual must be constructed from the same state fundamentals, selected environment, and relevant exogenous news inputs as the actual presidential estimate. Exclude both parties' candidate/VP effects and presidential-only campaign actions, faction effects, policy, and polling error from the counterfactual; exclude random polling noise from **both** sides. Use expected vote margins, not forecast win probabilities or election-night random draws. Audit actual model components before finalizing this decomposition; if a component cannot be consistently isolated, resolve that in design instead of treating the full presidential margin as the gap.

### One route to downballot races

The coattail component should affect the state generic ballot so its aggregate appears in the national environment, and the affected downballot races receive it through that same generic-ballot input. Record the underlying non-coattail environment and the coattail component separately; `stateGeneric` must equal their sum after bounds and `natGeneric` must equal the weighted state aggregate. When a separate independent generic effect changes the underlying environment, recompute the effective sum without reapplying past coattails. Attribute clipping consistently and preserve the current state bounds.

Audit and remove or adjust overlapping presidential terms in each downballot consumer. In particular, the existing Senate/governor use of presidential `marginOf` and the `stateMods` field contribution cannot remain as a second, unexamined copy of the same presidential movement. Keep independent race quality, incumbent, local news, downballot-specific campaign work, the prior Senate Democratic adjustment, district leans, and the existing legislature seat model; trace each by source. House districts in a state receive the same state-level coattail through their generic input. No district-lean or seat-count research or updates are in scope.

The same effective state generic value must feed House, Senate, governor, and state-legislature calculations, with their established race-specific fundamentals and explicitly identified **direct downballot** effects retained. Do not force each office's final margin to equal the generic ballot. Ensure forecast screens and Election Night use the same applicable race inputs, without adding a second forecast-only or result-only copy of coattails.

Avoid feedback loops: compute the presidential and counterfactual margins from the **non-coattail** generic environment for this calculation. The coattail contribution must not feed into its own input through `natGeneric`, `track`, or a downballot-to-president polling helper. Recalculate only from the pre-coattail snapshot and save the once-per-week receipt.

### Other parties and modes

Before enabling a comparable effect for a third-party nominee, design party-specific downballot shares and confirm how those races are modeled. Retain the selected build's four-party and regional-party rules until that design exists; in particular, preserve any approved mode that intentionally ignores the national environment. # Proposal: Fix Screen Jumps During Campaign Actions

**Status:** Forecast-related screen movement reproduced in the source app; fix not implemented or verified in a packaged game.

Check the shared action flow and other campaign actions. ## Diagnosis

### Confirmed source-app mechanism: forecast panel changes height twice

The inspected source has a concrete mechanism capable of producing the reported jump:

1. `src/pages/Home.jsx` commits an action by replacing the `game` object. The existing Build 39 fix captures the page's scroll and focus before the commit and restores them in `useLayoutEffect` immediately after that game update.
2. `src/lib/useElectionForecast.js` only returns the saved forecast when its stored `game` object is identical to the current one. As soon as an action supplies a new game object, the hook returns `forecast: null` and status `loading`. A new forecast request starts after a 60 ms delay and later completes asynchronously.
3. In `src/components/game/PredictionMeter.jsx`, `forecast: null` replaces the full Nick Bronze panel with a much shorter loading panel. When the new forecast arrives, the larger panel returns. That panel lives in `HudBar`, above the map and action controls in `GameScreen.jsx`.
4. The existing restoration has already run by the time the forecast completes. Preserving an absolute scroll offset also cannot keep a control in the same viewport position if content above it changes height. The visible content can move up and then down even if the numerical scroll position remains unchanged.

An isolated Microsoft Edge run against the source app reproduced this movement using an existing saved-campaign fixture with the pending event cleared. A scripted click activated the normal campaign-stop control. At **1440 × 900**, rallies in both Pennsylvania and Vermont changed the forecast panel from **159.5 px to 56.25 px**, a collapse of **103.25 px**. The map and rally control each moved upward by exactly 103.25 px with the page scroll position unchanged. Roughly 2.1–2.4 seconds after the click, the forecast returned to 159.5 px and both elements returned to their starting positions.

At **960 × 640**, the rally control likewise moved up 103.25 px in both states. After the forecast returned, the page scroll offset changed from 389 px to 492 px, leaving the control about 103 px above its original position. The narrow layout's map also changed position, though its movement differs because the two columns stack. The measurements establish a reproducible forecast-related jump in this source app; they do not establish that every reported jerk shares this cause, or that the shipped executable is identical. A volunteer-drive run did not reach an enabled action in the test fixture and does not establish a result for that action; town hall and packaged-app reproduction remain open. The test used a controlled existing fixture, not a fresh campaign.

Evidence and the repeatable script are in `outputs/screen-jump-diagnosis/measurements.json` and `outputs/screen-jump-diagnosis/measure-screen-jump.mjs`. The first test's fixture had $100M; later harness edits support additional action attempts. Do not assume the defect is specific to Pennsylvania or Vermont.

### Other confirmed gaps to investigate

- Town hall completion calls the action and then closes its panel (`GameScreen.jsx`), which changes document height. Town hall answer text can also appear during the interaction. A panel opening or closing may require normal, intentional reflow; determine whether it unexpectedly moves the user's working area.
- Decision events can appear after an action through `advanceDecisionEvent`; `EventCard` is inserted above the action bar. Its display and the player's ability to answer it are required. Do not remove or defer the event to avoid a jump.

A replaced container or a later asynchronous reflow is outside this mechanism. If an action is rejected and returns the same game object, the captured ref may remain pending until a later game update; establish whether that path is reachable and harmful before changing it.
- `handleSpin` and `handleAdvance` use separate state-update paths, while explicit staff and campaign-office navigation call `scrollIntoView`. Inventory all paths and distinguish intentional navigation from unintended movement. Preserve keyboard focus and deliberate navigation.
- The Build 39 regression uses mock viewport/scroll objects. The integration record says that suite passed, but the mock does not measure real rendered positions or asynchronous forecast updates. It does not establish that every packaged action screen is visually stable.

## Proposed fix

### 1. Keep the forecast footprint stable

Change the forecast lifecycle so that an ordinary campaign action does not collapse the forecast section and expand it again when the worker returns.

- Keep the most recent completed forecast visible while the replacement computes, with a clear, unobtrusive “Updating” status and accessible status announcement. Never present it as a current result after its underlying game state changes. Replace it with the new result when it arrives.
- For the first forecast, or after a genuine unavailable result with no prior forecast, render a responsive placeholder with the same structural footprint as the populated panel at that viewport width. The placeholder must stay readable at narrow widths and with long candidate names; reserve enough space for legitimate wrapping rather than imposing a fixed height that clips content.
- Discard obsolete worker responses so a slower older calculation cannot overwrite a newer game state. Preserve the existing simulation rules, forecast output, fallback/error behavior, and unrelated probability displays. Cancel or ignore outstanding work on unmount or campaign change; do not carry another campaign's forecast into the current campaign.
- If retaining the prior forecast would give misleading information in a particular mode or race transition, use the stable placeholder for that transition instead. Do not fabricate or cache a current-looking number.

This targets the confirmed shrink/expand mechanism. It should prevent the top of the campaign workspace from changing position solely because the forecast moved between loading and ready.

### 2. Preserve the working location without fighting the player

Any restoration must stop when the player intentionally scrolls or navigates and must not interfere with keyboard use.

Prefer eliminating unnecessary layout changes over repeated forced scrolling. If a remaining, reproduced jump needs anchoring, capture a stable visible control and restore its viewport-relative position only for the relevant action transaction; handle removed or shortened content by clamping naturally. Clear pending action snapshots when an action is rejected or a campaign/screen changes.
